# Landing Page Framework

## Mandatory Structure for ALL Landing Pages

Every landing page MUST follow this proven 9-block conversion framework, in this order. No exceptions. Each section answers one question, reduces one risk, and moves users toward one clear next step.

Sources: [involve.me](https://www.involve.me/blog/landing-page-structure), [digitalapplied.com](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-landing-pages-copy-templates-convert-guide), [tuffgrowth.com](https://tuffgrowth.com/high-converting-landing-page/)

## The 9-Block Framework

### 1. Navigation Bar
- Simplified — only essential links
- Include brand logo + primary CTA button in nav
- On service pages: use the same public nav as the homepage (consistency builds trust)
- Mobile: hamburger menu with same links

### 2. Hero Section (Above the Fold)
- **Headline**: Clear, benefit-driven, specific (not clever/vague)
- **Subheadline**: 1-2 sentences expanding on the promise
- **Primary CTA**: One clear action button (contrasting color)
- **Visual**: Relevant image, illustration, or video
- **Trust line**: Small proof element (e.g., "Trusted by 500+ investors" or "Starting at $5,000")
- Must pass the 5-second test: visitor knows what you offer, who it's for, and what to do next

### 3. Problem / Pain Section
- Acknowledge the visitor's pain point directly
- Use their language (not corporate jargon)
- Make them feel understood ("You're dealing with X and it's costing you Y")
- Optional: "Before vs After" or "Without vs With" comparison

### 4. Solution Section
- Present your service as THE answer to their problem
- Explain what you do in simple terms
- Show the transformation (from pain → desired outcome)
- Keep it concise — details come in the next section

### 5. Benefits / Features Section
- 3-6 key benefits with icons or visuals
- Lead with BENEFITS (what they get), not features (what it does)
- Use a grid layout for scannability
- Each benefit: icon + short title + 1-2 sentence description

### 6. Social Proof Section
- Testimonials (real names, photos if possible, specific results)
- Client logos or "As seen in" badges
- Statistics ("500+ businesses served", "98% satisfaction rate")
- Case study snippets or before/after results
- If no testimonials yet: use trust indicators (certifications, years of experience, guarantees)

### 7. How It Works / Process Section
- 3-4 numbered steps showing the journey
- Each step: number + title + brief description
- Reduces anxiety by showing what happens after they click
- Makes the service feel simple and structured

### 8. Pricing / Offer Section (if applicable)
- Clear pricing or "Starting at $X"
- What's included (checklist format)
- Comparison table if multiple tiers
- Money-back guarantee or risk-reversal statement

### 9. Final CTA Section
- Repeat the primary call-to-action
- Add urgency or scarcity if genuine ("Limited spots", "Book this week")
- Include secondary option (phone number, email) for those not ready to commit
- Brief reassurance ("No obligation", "Free consultation", "Cancel anytime")

### 10. Footer
- Contact info (phone, email, address)
- Quick links
- Legal (privacy, terms)
- Social media links

## Design Rules

- **One primary CTA** repeated 2-3 times throughout the page (hero, mid-page, final)
- **Contrasting CTA button color** — must stand out from the page background
- **White space** — generous padding between sections (py-16 to py-24)
- **Visual hierarchy** — headings are large and bold, body text is readable (16px+)
- **Mobile-first** — every section must look great at 375px
- **Consistent brand** — use the site's existing color palette and typography
- **No navigation distractions** — simplified nav, no sidebar, no competing links

## Copy Rules

- Headlines: 6-12 words, benefit-driven
- Subheadlines: 15-25 words, expand on the promise
- Body: Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)
- Bullet points for lists (easier to scan than paragraphs)
- Use "you" language (talk TO the visitor, not ABOUT yourself)
- Include specific numbers when possible ("Save 45 minutes per deal", not "Save time")

## When Building a Landing Page

1. Start with the 9-block structure
2. Write the copy FIRST (headlines, body, CTAs)
3. Then build the layout around the copy
4. Include the site's public navigation at the top
5. Include a footer with contact info
6. Test at mobile (375px), tablet (768px), and desktop (1440px)
7. Verify no JS errors with Playwright

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ No navigation (visitor feels trapped)
- ❌ Multiple competing CTAs (confuses the visitor)
- ❌ No social proof (no reason to trust you)
- ❌ Feature-heavy, benefit-light (nobody cares about features, they care about outcomes)
- ❌ Wall of text (nobody reads paragraphs on landing pages)
- ❌ Generic stock photos (reduces trust)
- ❌ No clear next step (visitor doesn't know what to do)
